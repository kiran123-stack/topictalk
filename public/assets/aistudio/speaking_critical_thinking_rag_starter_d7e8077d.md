# Speaking & Critical Thinking Topic Generator --- RAG Starter Knowledge Base

Version: 1.0 Primary context: India, with global comparisons Purpose:
Help an AI generate specific, balanced speaking prompts for MBA
interviews, college debates, group discussions, UPSC preparation, and
spontaneous speaking practice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. How to use this knowledge base

Use this document as background knowledge, not as a script to copy.
Retrieve the most relevant sections for each request.

For every generated topic: - Ask one specific, debatable question. -
Give enough context for a student to understand the issue without
specialist knowledge. - Include at least two reasonable perspectives. -
Encourage the speaker to form and defend an independent position. -
Prefer concrete policies, trade-offs, stakeholders, and real-world
consequences over broad themes. - Do not present illustrative examples
in this document as current facts without checking an authoritative,
up-to-date source.

This document is a starter corpus. Add current official reports, policy
documents, and recent explainers as separate files.

## 3. Topic construction patterns

Use these patterns to create fresh prompts. Do not repeatedly reuse the
same wording.

### A. Policy trade-off

A government has limited resources. Should it prioritise intervention A
or intervention B? Who benefits, who bears the cost, and what evidence
should guide the choice?

### B. Intended outcome versus actual outcome

A policy is designed to solve a particular problem. What conditions must
exist for it to work, and how could it fail in practice?

### C. Rights versus public interest

When should individual freedom be limited to protect public safety or
collective welfare? Who should decide, and what safeguards are needed?

### D. Short-term versus long-term

Should decision-makers prioritise immediate relief or long-term
investment? What happens if either is neglected?

### E. Equality versus efficiency

Should a system prioritise equal access, merit-based selection, or
measurable outcomes? Can these goals be reconciled?

### F. Technology and society

Does a new technology solve the underlying problem, or merely shift
costs and risks to another group?

### G. Accountability

Who should be held responsible when a public or private institution
makes a harmful decision?

### H. Local versus national priorities

Should policy be uniform across the country, or adapted to local needs
and conditions?

### I. Ethical dilemma

A decision produces a beneficial outcome for many but imposes a serious
cost on a smaller group. Is it justified?

### J. Institutional reform

Should an existing institution be improved gradually, redesigned, or
replaced? What risks accompany each option?

## 4. Illustrative speaking-topic bank

These are examples of the required specificity and quality. They are not
a list to repeat verbatim.

### Public policy and government schemes

-   Should cash assistance to families be linked to school attendance,
    or should welfare remain unconditional?
-   Can a scheme designed to empower girls succeed if safety, school
    quality, and employment opportunities remain weak?
-   Should governments publish simple, comparable outcome reports for
    every major welfare scheme?
-   When public funds are limited, should a state prioritise universal
    services or targeted subsidies?

### Economy and employment

-   Should India focus more on creating formal jobs or supporting small
    self-employed businesses?
-   Is a rising GDP enough to judge whether ordinary households are
    becoming better off?
-   Should gig workers receive benefits similar to those available to
    regular employees?
-   Should governments tax consumption more lightly even if this reduces
    revenue for public services?

### Education

-   Should university admission place more weight on entrance
    examinations or school performance?
-   Should colleges make internships compulsory if good placements are
    not equally available to all students?
-   Does attendance measure learning, or mainly measure physical
    presence?
-   Should public universities prioritise affordable access even if that
    limits spending per student?

### Gender and society

-   Are financial incentives sufficient to improve the education and
    independence of girls?
-   Should parental leave policies be designed to encourage fathers to
    take more responsibility for childcare?
-   How should society balance personal choice with persistent social
    expectations around marriage and careers?

### Technology and AI

-   Should employers be required to disclose when AI is used to screen
    job applicants?
-   If AI makes some professional tasks cheaper, should the productivity
    gains benefit workers, firms, or consumers?
-   Should schools allow generative AI in assignments if students must
    explain and defend their work orally?
-   Who should be responsible when an automated decision unfairly
    rejects a loan or job applicant?

### Environment and cities

-   Should cities restrict private vehicles even when public transport
    is unreliable?
-   Should electricity prices reflect environmental costs if that makes
    energy more expensive for low-income households?
-   Is it fair to ask developing regions to limit industrial growth to
    meet climate targets?

### Democracy, rights, and institutions

-   Should elected representatives face stronger penalties for making
    demonstrably false public claims?
-   How can a democracy protect free speech while limiting coordinated
    disinformation?
-   Should local governments receive more financial autonomy, even if
    this creates uneven service quality?

### Business and management

-   Should companies prioritise employee well-being when it conflicts
    with short-term profitability?
-   Should platforms be responsible for the working conditions of
    independent contractors who depend on them?
-   Is rapid business growth a meaningful success measure if a company
    cannot demonstrate a sustainable path to profitability?

### International relations

-   Should countries prioritise economic self-reliance or deeper global
    trade integration?
-   When should a country take a public position on another country's
    internal conflict?
-   Can global climate agreements be fair when countries have
    contributed unequally to historical emissions?

## 5. Speaking and reasoning framework

Encourage users to structure a response with this flexible sequence:

1.  **Position:** State the central view in one clear sentence.
2.  **Reason:** Explain the strongest reason behind that view.
3.  **Evidence or example:** Use a relevant policy, event, observation,
    or credible data point.
4.  **Counterargument:** Acknowledge the strongest opposing case.
5.  **Evaluation:** Explain why the chosen position still holds, or what
    condition would change it.
6.  **Conclusion:** End with a practical, balanced takeaway.

Do not force this structure on every answer. For short speaking drills,
a simple claim-reason-example-conclusion structure is enough.

## 6. Critical-thinking lenses

When useful, ask the speaker to examine: - Stakeholders: Who benefits
and who loses? - Incentives: How might people change their behaviour in
response? - Implementation: What could go wrong between policy design
and delivery? - Evidence: What information would confirm or challenge
the claim? - Trade-offs: What must be sacrificed to achieve the desired
outcome? - Fairness: Is the burden distributed fairly? - Scale: Would
the idea work in a village, city, state, and across India? - Time: What
are the immediate and long-term effects? - Unintended consequences: What
second-order effects could emerge? - Alternatives: Is there a better way
to achieve the same goal?

## 7. Difficulty guidance

**Beginner:** Familiar issues, clearly identifiable stakeholders, and
everyday examples.

**Intermediate:** Public policy trade-offs, competing priorities,
implementation problems, and economic or social consequences.

**Advanced:** Constitutional principles, institutional design,
conflicting rights, uncertain evidence, multi-stakeholder negotiations,
and long-term consequences.

Difficulty should reflect the reasoning required, not obscure
vocabulary.

## 9. Source and factuality rules

-   For current schemes, laws, office-holders, budgets, statistics, and
    recent events, retrieve the latest relevant source before stating
    details.
-   Record the source title, publisher, publication or update date, URL,
    and date accessed.
-   Distinguish a proposal, announcement, approved scheme, active
    implementation, and measured outcome.
-   Do not treat a press release as independent proof that a scheme
    achieved its stated goal.
-   Do not invent statistics, quotations, legal provisions, or scheme
    eligibility rules.
-   If reliable evidence is unavailable, frame the issue as a discussion
    question without asserting the disputed claim.
-   Present competing arguments fairly and distinguish evidence from
    opinion.

## 10. Non-repetition and memory

The model cannot reliably guarantee non-repetition from this document
alone.

The application should store each generated topic in a persistent
topic-history database with: - topic text - category - key issue - main
stakeholders - central dilemma or trade-off - generation date

Before generating a new topic, retrieve recent history and reject: -
exact duplicates - paraphrases of previous topics - topics with the same
central dilemma and stakeholders

Prefer a new combination of category, stakeholder, policy mechanism, and
trade-off.

## 11. Suggested metadata for retrieved documents

When adding documents to the knowledge base, attach metadata where
supported:

-   `country`: India / Global / State-specific
-   `category`: one taxonomy category
-   `source_type`: official / legislative / statistical / research /
    explainer
-   `publisher`: issuing institution
-   `published_date`: date on the source
-   `last_updated`: latest update date, if available
-   `topic_tags`: concise subject tags
-   `validity`: current / historical / needs verification

Retrieve the most relevant chunks, not the entire knowledge base for
every request.
